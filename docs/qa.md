# Prepared answers for the Q&A

Nine questions we expect after the talk, each with a prepared answer. Everyone answers in the Q&A, so read all nine before the presentation.

**Why would anyone scan a coffee cup?**

Seven in ten Americans already scan codes every month, most often in restaurants. This one answers a question record prices have made common: where does my money go?

**Isn't this Starbucks' tracing tool again?**

That tool runs one way and covers bagged beans. Cup2Farm adds proof of payment at farmer level and a channel back to the grower.

**What if only 1% of cups get scanned?**

The pilot gates on scan rate, so we learn that cheaply. Even at 1%, cafes get a trust feature that sets Coda apart in wholesale.

**Does showing pay put farmers at risk?**

Amounts are never shown. Drinkers see a co-op level premium, and profiles are opt-in with a first name and district only.

**Why not a native app with loyalty points?**

About 3% of food and drink app users remain after 30 days, and points would turn a benevolent app into a push app. Loyalty can live in another area of the app.

**What does it cost, and who pays?**

We model $120,000 to $240,000 a year, plus Bext360's fee of 1 to 2% of the wholesale bean price. Year one is an investment; break-even comes with partner reach.

**What if a lot cannot be traced?**

The app says so. Part of Coda's coffee comes through Fair Trade co-ops or client-supplied beans, and showing the gap is part of being trusted.

**How will you know it worked?**

A trust survey before and after, against a control group, the design Urban and Sultan used. Scan and thank-you rates are tracked weekly.

**Does PFG's ownership change the plan?**

It widens it. PFG's network puts Coda in more places without its name on the cup, which is the gap the code closes.
